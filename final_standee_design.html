SceneNarrator
Scene Narration + Navigation for the Visually Impaired
Benchmark evaluation of Vision–Language Models and LoRA fine-tuning for assistive AI in Pakistani indoor environments.
Abstract
State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models are mainly evaluated on Western-centric datasets, limiting their effectiveness in developing regions. This work introduces PakVLM-Bench, a localized benchmark of 400 real-world Pakistani images from supermarkets and homes. Three open VLMs were evaluated in zero-shot settings, with LoRA fine-tuning of LLaVA showing notable gains in narration, spatial grounding, and hallucination reduction.
Problem Statement
Evaluating egocentric vision maps $f:(I, Q) \rightarrow R$ for Visual Impairment Assistance (VIA) in unstructured Pakistani retail and home settings exposes trade-offs between linguistic fluency and spatial/navigational precision. Modern VLMs fail as specialized tactile assistants due to crucial constraints:
Visual Grounding: Preventing factual hallucinations.
Semantic Accuracy: Lacking human tactical reasoning.
Functional Utility: Missing safe, navigable instructions.
Research Objectives
RO1: Test if general benchmarks translate to localized zero-shot and fine-tuned assistive navigation.
RO2: Measure how effectively lexical and semantic metrics capture actual assistive utility.
RO3: Evaluate reference-free and hallucination-aware metrics for better consistency indicators.
RO4: Deploy a curated regional benchmark dataset to expose regional model limitations.

Contributions
01.
Localized Pakistani benchmark dataset:
400 images from supermarkets and homes with narrative-style ground truth, developed using human-in-the-loop annotation.
02.
Proposed unified multi-level evaluation framework:
First-of-its-kind pipeline combining lexical, semantic, visual-grounding, and hallucination metrics applied to an assistive AI task.
03.
LoRA fine-tuning for domain adaptation:
Fine-tuning of LLaVA-1.5-7B on 650 instruction pairs yielding consistent gains across all evaluation dimensions with reduced hallucination rate.

Limitations & Future Work
Limitations
Dataset scale limits generalisability across edge-cases.
Evaluation conducted in offline settings; real-time latency, and user interaction not tested.
Future Work
Expand dataset to rural markets, and transport hubs.
Integrate monocular depth estimation for obstacle distance.
Deploy fine-tuned model on edge devices for real-time assistive use.
